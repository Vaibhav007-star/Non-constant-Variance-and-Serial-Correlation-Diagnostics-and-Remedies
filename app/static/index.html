Foundations of Econometric Diagnostics

1. The Gauss-Markov Assumptions

In a linear regression model \( Y = X\beta + \epsilon \), the Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) estimator \( \hat{\beta} = (X'X)^{-1}X'Y \) is the Best Linear Unbiased Estimator (BLUE) if:

  • Homoscedasticity: \( \text{Var}(\epsilon_i \mid X) = \sigma^2 \) (constant variance across all observations).
  • Error Independence: \( \text{Cov}(\epsilon_t, \epsilon_s \mid X) = 0 \) for \( t \neq s \) (no serial correlation).

2. Consequences of Violations

When either assumption is violated:

  • \( \hat{\beta}_{OLS} \) remains unbiased and consistent (provided \( \mathbb{E}[\epsilon \mid X] = 0 \)).
  • OLS is inefficient: it no longer possesses minimum variance.
  • The classical covariance matrix \( \widehat{\text{Var}}(\hat{\beta}) = s^2(X'X)^{-1} \) is biased and invalid, producing misleading t-statistics, false confidence intervals, and spurious discoveries.

3. Sandwich Estimators & Robust Covariance

White's Heteroscedasticity-Consistent (HC) covariance estimators replace the homoscedastic assumption with a robust "sandwich":

\[ \widehat{\text{Var}}_{\text{sandwich}}(\hat{\beta}) = (X'X)^{-1} \left( \sum_{i=1}^n w_i e_i^2 x_i x_i' \right) (X'X)^{-1} \]

HC3 (MacKinnon & White, 1985): Sets \( w_i = (1 - h_{ii})^{-2} \), weighting by leverage \( h_{ii} = x_i'(X'X)^{-1}x_i \), providing optimal small-sample properties.

4. Newey-West HAC Covariance

When both heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation exist, the Newey-West (1987) estimator introduces Bartlett kernel weights to guarantee positive semi-definiteness:

\[ \hat{\Omega}_{\text{HAC}} = \hat{\Gamma}_0 + \sum_{j=1}^m \left( 1 - \frac{j}{m+1} \right) (\hat{\Gamma}_j + \hat{\Gamma}_j') \]
